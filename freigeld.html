Wörgl issued these in July 1932 at 1% a month. Holders passed them on nine to ten times faster than the national schilling; the town paved roads and built a bridge while unemployment fell 16% against a 19% rise across Austria. Austria's central bank shut it down in 1933. American towns copied it at 2% a week — and the money was simply refused.
